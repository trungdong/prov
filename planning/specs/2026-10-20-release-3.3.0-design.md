# Release 3.3.0: core design (spec 1 of 3)

**Date:** 2026-10-20, revised 2026-10-21 after two independent reviews
**Status:** Draft; decisions D1 to D12 settled 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-21
**Scope:** The Python 3.11 floor, the structural work the conversions need, every
3.2.3 `ProvFutureRejectionWarning` turned into its 3.3.0 behaviour, one form for
IRI and qualified-name attribute values, and four fixes that are ready. The
equality, typing, model and feature items on the milestone wait for specs 2 and
3, written against what this one delivers (section 2).

## 1. Purpose

3.2.3 (2026-10-19) warns about every input that 3.3.0 rejects or reads
differently. Each message says what 3.3.0 does and names the issue that tracks
the rule, and the [Prepare for prov 3.3.0](../../docs/howto/prepare-for-3-3.md)
guide lists them by area. The support policy in `ROADMAP.md` drops Python 3.10
in the first release after its end of life on 2026-10-31. 3.3.0 is the release
that keeps both promises, so it cannot ship before 2026-11-01 and should ship
soon after.

The 3.3.0 milestone holds 51 open issues. This spec takes 32 of them, in four
groups:

- **The floor** (section 3). Python 3.11 everywhere the project names a version.
- **Structure** (section 4). #589, #591 and #587, three refactors with no
  behaviour change that the conversions need first: `provjson.py` is at the
  complexity limit, `records.py` is at 2,000 lines, and the unresolved-name
  check exists three times.
- **Conversions** (section 5). The twenty warned issues (#472, #482, #508,
  #509, #510, #511, #513, #516, #519, #523, #524, #528, #529, #531, #532,
  #547, #551, #552, #553, #555), #590 which tracks them, #588, #597 and the
  second half of #526, 24 in all. Each warning site raises or changes behaviour as its message
  promised, its tests become tests of the new behaviour, and its section of
  the how-to is rewritten in the present tense. #588 removes the machinery
  that kept copies from warning again.
- **Ready fixes** (section 6). #564 and #545, #569, #586 and #599. Each is
  decided or follows from a conversion.

Every other issue on the milestone is listed in section 2 with the spec it
waits for.

**The compatibility promise.** A user who silenced every 3.2.3 warning should
read the same documents on 3.3.0. Two reviews on 2026-10-21 found the places
where this spec breaks that promise without a prior warning. Each is kept
because the alternative leaves a conformance gap, and each is listed in
`HISTORY.md` under its own heading (section 7.2) and in the how-to:

- a Python `datetime` with an offset beyond ±14:00 as an ordinary attribute
  value (5.4, D4);
- time text outside the `xsd:dateTime` lexical form that
  `datetime.fromisoformat` reads, such as `2012-12-03T21:08Z`, the basic
  format and week dates, and on CPython the text that only the 3.10 fraction
  padding let through, such as `2012-12-03T21:08:16.5 +01:00` (5.4, D13);
- the full-IRI positions PR #581 listed as still silent (5.8, D3);
- an `Identifier` attribute value, stored as an `xsd:anyURI` `Literal` from
  3.3.0, and a resolving `xsd:QName` literal, stored as a `QualifiedName`
  (5.7, D9);
- an `xsi:` qualified name outside PROV-XML with no `xsi` declared (5.1, D10);
- a repeated key in a PROV-JSONLD object (5.10);
- under the `lenient` PROV-N profile, a statement-level rejection that becomes
  a skip (5.3, D1).

Section 9 adds the test that would have caught these: documents written by
3.2.3 in every format, read under 3.3.0.

## 2. Specs 2 and 3

The milestone is planned as three sequenced specs, and the release is cut after
the first. Anything from spec 2 that is merged by the cut ships in 3.3.0; the
rest rolls to the next minor, and the `ROADMAP.md` row is edited once, in the
release PR, to say what moved. The later specs are written against what this
one delivered, because their rulings depend on it.

**Spec 2, equality and typing.** Record and document equality (#525, #549 with
the outside PR #582), literal equality and lexical forms (#527, #593, #594,

3.2.3's `HISTORY.md` says equal identifier-less records "stay two, until
`unified()` removes duplicates in 3.3.0 (#548)". #548 deduplicates on `==`,
and spec 2 changes `==`, so landing it first would move its result twice. It
rolls with the rest of spec 2 (D11), and 3.3.0's `HISTORY.md` says so under
the unification heading.

**Spec 3, model and features.** The immutability guard (#444), parent-aware
namespace reconciliation (#449, which #599's prefix-lookup rule in section 5.8
makes simpler), the PROV-N reader and writer refactors (#450, whose `xsi`
special-casing goes in section 5.1), the identifier equal to the default
namespace IRI (#473, a writer design across four formats, D12), wrapped PROV-N
output (#131), extensibility expressions (#520), the Pygments PROV-N lexer, the
structural `diff()` for `prov-compare`, the typed-literal dispatch proposed on
#589, and the remaining 3.2.1 review items. #336 stays deferred as its own text
says.

**Checkpoint.** The policy floor creates the gap: 3.3.0 cannot ship before
2026-11-01, and the fourteen build PRs are expected to merge before then.
Between PR 13 and the release PR (section 8):

1. Write spec 2 against the merged code and take its rulings. The typing stream
   (#491, #492, #494) is additive and starts at once; the equality rulings
   decide what else is small enough to land before the cut.
2. The equality stream (#525, #527, #549, #593 to #595) lands whole or not at
   all, because each changes `==` with no warning and downstream test suites

3. Freeze. After the freeze commit nothing merges before the release PR. Run
   the release-wide A/B against `3.2.3` and the release-wide `/code-review
   high` on that commit (section 7.3).
4. Cut 3.3.0 on 2026-11-01, or when the review is clear if later.

Spec 3 is written after 3.3.0 ships: #449 builds on section 5.8's rule, #450
on 5.1, and the speed work wants the `3.3.0` tag as its A/B baseline. Whatever
rolls out of 3.3.0 lands in the next minor, which `ROADMAP.md` gives to the
recording helpers today; whether they share the release or move to 3.5.0 is

### 4.3 One unresolved-name checker (#587)

### 5.1 PROV-N declarations (#508, #511, #510, #519 a and d)

**3.3.0.** `ProvNSyntaxError` at the declaration for a repeated `default`
(#508), a prefix that is not a `PN_PREFIX` (#511), and under `strict` a
`default` after a `prefix` line or a redeclaration of `prov` or `xsd` at the
predeclared IRI (#519).

**`xsi` is PROV-XML's (D10, #510).** In the PROV-XML Recommendation `xsi:type`
is the XML Schema Instance attribute that carries a value's datatype, declared
by the document's own `xmlns:xsi`; the XML codec reads it as the datatype and
writes it the same way, and it never becomes a model-level `xsi:` name.
PROV-N, PROV-JSON, PROV-JSONLD and PROV-O define no `xsi` prefix. So `xsi`
leaves `DEFAULT_NAMESPACES` (`namespaces.py:12`, a public dict whose contents
change; `_DEFAULT_NAMESPACES_BY_URI` and the manager seeding at `:39` and `:76`
follow); the XML codec uses the `XSI` constant from `prov.constants`
(`provxml.py:192` and `_ns_xsi` at `:761`, which would otherwise `KeyError`);
the parser's special cases (`_xsi_name` at `provn_parser.py:408-419` and its
callers at 741, 761, 825, 838, 883) and the PROV-N writer's `_uses_xsi()` scan
(`bundle.py:637-711`) go. A `QualifiedName` built from `prov.constants.XSI`
registers its namespace when it is asserted as an identifier, an attribute
name or a value, as any namespace does, so the writer declares it like any
prefix. The one position that registers nothing is a `Literal`'s datatype:
no writer but RDF declares the namespace of a datatype that was never
registered, and no reader reads the output back (probed 2026-10-21 on every
format), a defect older than `xsi`'s special case and tracked as #615; PR 6
drops the `xsi`-only cover for it. An undeclared `xsi:` name in
PROV-N is then an ordinary unresolved name under section 5.8's rule, which is
what 3.2.3 D13 warned about, and #510 closes in PR 6. The XML reader resolves
`xsi:type` by key and registers no namespace for it, so a document read from
PROV-XML gains no `prefix xsi` line in PROV-N. One visible output change: a
PROV-N document that declares `prefix xsi <http://other/>` is written back
today under `xsi_1`, because the built-in `xsi` held the prefix; from 3.3.0
it keeps its own name. `HISTORY.md` lists it under output changes.

An `xsi:` qualified name asserted in code or read from PROV-JSON or
PROV-JSONLD with no `xsi` declared reads today without a warning, because the
model's manager found the prefix built in. From 3.3.0 the model rejects it,
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName` for an attribute name and
`ProvElementIdentifierRequired` for an identifier, and PROV-JSON and
PROV-JSONLD files that 3.2.x wrote with `xsi:` names carry no `xsi` prefix
entry, so the how-to tells the reader to add one. RDF rejects nothing: its
names are full IRIs, and only the prefix the reader generates for the XML
Schema Instance namespace changes. The PROV-XML writer raises
`ProvXMLException` for a document that binds `xsi` to another IRI, because
`xsi:type` fixes that prefix in the output; 3.2.x renamed such a prefix to
`xsi_1`. Each goes on the `HISTORY.md` unwarned list with a how-to row.

**Site.** The declaration methods of `provn_parser.py` (lines 346 to 418) and
`_check_reserved_prefix` (378). Line 305, the parse loop, is section 5.3's.

**Tests.** `test_provn.py`, `test_provn_profiles.py`, `test_xml.py` (16 `xsi:`
uses, unchanged in behaviour), `test_public_api.py`. A 3.2.2 PROV-N file with
an undeclared `xsi:type` fails with a message that names the missing
declaration; the how-to tells the user to read and write it again with 3.2.3.

### 5.3 PROV-N statements (#523, #519 b and c; #586, #569)

**3.3.0.** `ProvNSyntaxError` for an attribute named after the statement's own
formal argument (#523), and under `strict` for a statement after a bundle and
for an identifier or attribute block on `alternateOf`, `specializationOf` or
`hadMember` (#519). Each check raises where it recorded a message, so the
first error ends the parse. The parser's deferred `self.warnings` list goes
with its last caller, the value check of section 5.8 (PR 10).

**Lenient profile (D1).** A rejection raised while parsing a statement skips
that statement with the existing skip `ProvWarning`, as a syntax error inside a
statement does today: `_statement` (`provn_parser.py:496-515`) catches
`ProvNSyntaxError` and `(ProvException, ValueError, OverflowError)` and routes
both through `_skip_or_raise()`, which also catches `ProvValueError` and
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName`. A declaration or lexer rejection runs
outside `_statement` (lines 287 and 293) and raises under every profile. Today
`_check_formal_attributes` runs after `new_record()` (lines 641 to 642), an
order 3.2.3 chose so a model-rejected statement did not also warn; the PR
moves the call before `new_record()`, otherwise a lenient read would report
the statement skipped while the record stayed in the bundle. The skip message
carries the error's text, the detail 3.2.3 warned with and no issue number, as
every 3.3.0 error message does (D7); a 3.2.3 filter on
`ProvFutureRejectionWarning` cannot match a skip, which is a `ProvWarning`.
The how-to's PROV-N sections and `HISTORY.md` say that under
`lenient` a statement-level rejection is a skip, since a 3.2.3 user who
validated with `-W error` under `lenient` sees a different outcome.

This answers #586 with no per-statement buffer once the value check of
section 5.8 raises too (PR 10). Until then a `lenient` read of a statement
that both names an argument and holds an undeclared value prefix warns about
the value and then skips the statement, so #586 closes with PR 10.

**Strict writer (D2, #569).** `get_provn(strict=True)` (`bundle.py:683`) drops
the identifier and the attributes of these three relations and emits a
`ProvWarning` naming the record and what was dropped, so its output reads
under `strict`. The docstring says so. Since 3.2.3's #530 fan-out gives every
member of a multi-member PROV-JSON `hadMember` the statement's identifier and
attributes, this case is common on PROV-JSON input, so `HISTORY.md` names the
writer as lossy under `strict=True` and the how-to gets a row under "PROV-N
statements". This lands in the same PR, before the strict reader rejects.

**Strict profile.** `prov` already refuses a bare `mentionOf` and `-` in a
required position under `strict` (`_classify` at `provn_parser.py:567-572`;
`_check_strict_required_positions`); the PR records that in
`docs/reference/conformance.md`.

**Site.** `_check_formal_attributes` (`provn_parser.py:656`),
`_check_strict_bare_relation` (643), the parse loop (305), and the writer's
relation output.

**Tests.** `test_provn.py`, `test_provn_profiles.py`; the #569 reproduction
round-trips under `strict` with one warning per dropped item; a lenient read of
a statement with an attribute named after its argument skips it with one
warning. The undeclared value prefix case is PR 10's.

### 5.4 Times (#513)

**3.3.0.** A time with a timezone offset outside −14:00 to +14:00 is rejected
where the time is built, from any reader or from code, with `ProvValueError`
or the reader's error. XSD 1.1 (§3.3.7) bounds the offset to ±14:00, so such a
value has no `xsd:dateTime` form. The check applies to every `datetime` value
asserted on a record, in a time position or as an ordinary attribute value
(D4), because the writers would otherwise emit a time no reader accepts. An
`xsd:dateTime` typed literal whose text does not parse stays an opaque
`Literal` (3.2.3 D5).

The attribute-value case had no 3.2.3 warning, because the 3.2.3 bound applied
only where a time was built (the maintainer's note on #513, 2026-10-18).
`HISTORY.md` names it as a rejection without a prior warning. The how-to's
Times section now says "An `xsd:dateTime` value of any other attribute is not
a time of the statement, so 3.3.0 does not reject it"; the rewrite separates
the typed literal, kept opaque, from a Python `datetime`, rejected in any
position, so the sentence is not read as a promise 3.3.0 breaks.

**Time text that is not an `xsd:dateTime`.** `parse_xsd_datetime` reads time
text with `datetime.fromisoformat`, which accepts ISO 8601 forms outside the
`xsd:dateTime` lexical space, such as `2012-12-03T21:08Z`, `20121203T210816Z`,
`2012-W01-1T21:08:16Z` and a comma before the fraction. The 3.10 padding of a
fraction to six digits let CPython read a few more, such as
`2012-12-03T21:08:16.5 +01:00` and `2012-12-03T21:40.8:16-00:00`, which PyPy
never did. From 3.3.0, `parse_xsd_datetime` matches the text against the XSD
lexical form `-?YYYY(Y*)-MM-DDThh:mm:ss(.s+)?(Z|(+|-)hh:mm)?` (XSD 1.1 §3.3.7)
before calling `fromisoformat`, and text outside it takes each reader's path

### 5.7 Python values (#482) and one form for IRI and qualified-name values (#526, D9)

**3.3.0.** An attribute value of a type with no PROV form raises
`ProvValueError` at assertion. A value of a type in the 3.2.3 how-to table is
stored as a `Literal` of its datatype (3.2.3 D15): `Decimal` as `xsd:decimal`,
`date` as `xsd:date`, `time` as `xsd:time`, `timedelta` as `xsd:duration`,
`UUID` as `xsd:anyURI`, `bytes`, `bytearray` and `memoryview` as
`xsd:base64Binary`. A converted value reads back as the `Literal`. The lexical
forms: a `Decimal` as `format(value, "f")`, and `NaN` or an infinity, which
have no `xsd:decimal` form, raise; a `date` and a `time` by `isoformat()`, and
a `time` whose offset is not a whole number of minutes raises; a `timedelta`
as an `xsd:duration` of days, hours, minutes and seconds with the sign in
front and `PT0S` for zero; a `UUID` as `urn:uuid:…`; `bytes`, `bytearray` and
`memoryview` as standard base64. `AttributeValue` names `Identifier` and the
six converted types; `SupportedXSDParsedTypes` and `CoercedAttributeValue`
lose `Identifier`.

**One form (D9).** Two kinds of value have two forms today, and which one a
document holds depends on the format it last passed through.

- An `Identifier` value and a `Literal(uri, XSD_ANYURI)` are both stored as
  `Identifier` (`XSD_DATATYPE_PARSERS[XSD_ANYURI] = Identifier`,
  `records.py:244`; `Identifier` is in `_PLAIN_VALUE_TYPES`, `:741`), every
  writer emits either as `xsd:anyURI` (`provjson.py:1018`, `provjsonld.py:184`,
  `provxml.py:561`, PROV-N as a typed literal, `provrdf.py:539` as a typed
  literal), and every reader builds an `Identifier` back. PROV-N production 41
  makes `"…" %% xsd:anyURI` a literal.
- A `Literal("ex:x", XSD_QNAME)` is kept opaque by the model
  (`_auto_literal_conversion`, `records.py:1042-1055`, citing #168) and by the
  PROV-N and RDF readers, but the PROV-JSON, PROV-JSONLD and XML readers
  resolve it to a `QualifiedName`, so `doc == reloaded` fails through those
  three formats (probe on `main`, 2026-10-21). A `prov:QUALIFIED_NAME` literal
  already becomes a `QualifiedName` at assertion in every path.

From 3.3.0 each has one form, decided at assertion in
`_coerce_attribute_value()`:

- an `Identifier` value that is not a `QualifiedName` (`QualifiedName`
  subclasses `Identifier`, and `_auto_literal_conversion` tests it first at
  `records.py:1040`) becomes `Literal(uri, XSD_ANYURI)`, and every reader
  builds that `Literal` from `xsd:anyURI`; the reader entries that built an
  `Identifier` (the `datatype == XSD_ANYURI` branch of
  `decode_json_representation`, `XSD_DATATYPE_PARSERS[XSD_ANYURI]`) go,
  `XSD_DATATYPE_PARSERS` loses the key and `SupportedXSDParsedTypes` narrows
  (public tables whose contents change; no name leaves `dir(prov.model)`).
  Writer output is unchanged in every format, including RDF, which already
  wrote `"…"^^xsd:anyURI`. D15's UUID conversion assumes this form.
- a `Literal` typed `xsd:QName` or `prov:QUALIFIED_NAME` that resolves becomes
  a `QualifiedName`, in every path; one that does not resolve is rejected
  (section 5.8). The PROV-N writer then writes `'ex:x'` where it wrote
  `"ex:x" %% xsd:QName`, and the RDF writer writes the IRI where it wrote
  `"ex:x"^^xsd:QName`, which is what PROV-O says a qualified name is. Because a
  `QualifiedName` value registers its namespace on the bundle through
  `_reconcile_qualified_name`, copies made by `unified()`, `flattened()`,
  `update()` and `add_record()` carry the prefix and the writers declare it;
  this closes the gap the review found, where a resolving `xsd:QName` literal
  registered nothing, so copies re-checked it against an empty manager and
  PROV-JSON output declared no prefix for it.

**Why the two conversions run in opposite directions.** A qualified-name
value denotes a resource: PROV-DM defines a qualified name as an identifier
that maps to an IRI, PROV-O writes it as that IRI, and `"ex:x" %% xsd:QName`
is one of two spellings of it, `'ex:x'` being the other. Resolving it into the
model's resource type drops a spelling and nothing else. An `xsd:anyURI` value
denotes a string whose lexical form is a URI: XSD places it in the literal
value space, PROV-N production 41 makes it a typed literal, and in RDF
`"http://x"^^xsd:anyURI` and `<http://x>` are different terms that give
different graphs. Promoting it to an `Identifier` would turn data into a
resource. The `Identifier` attribute value itself has been a half-type: the
model treated it as a bare IRI resource, but PROV-N and PROV-JSON carry a
resource value only as a qualified name, so every writer, the RDF writer
included, has emitted it as an `xsd:anyURI` literal. D9 makes the model say
what its output has always said. A caller who wants an IRI as a resource
value uses a `QualifiedName` under a declared namespace, which every format
writes as a resource. The RDF reader mints a namespace for an IRI object no
declared namespace compacts (`_resolve_iri`), so it never builds an
`Identifier`. The PROV-JSONLD reader builds `Literal(iri, XSD_ANYURI)` for a
`{"@id": …}` node reference no namespace compacts, where it built an
`Identifier`; the writer's output for that value, an `xsd:anyURI` value object,
is unchanged. `prov.dot` renders an `xsd:anyURI` `Literal` as its text with a
link, as it rendered the `Identifier`. The writers' `Identifier` branches and
`Identifier.provn_representation` have no caller once no stored value is a
bare `Identifier`; the branches go, and the public method stays until the
checkpoint judges it.

Both are stored-type changes with no 3.2.3 warning: `isinstance(value,
Identifier)` in user code stops matching, and two writer outputs change.
`HISTORY.md` lists them under the unwarned heading; the how-to gets a row
under "Python values" and one under "Qualified names".

**Site.** `records.py:800` and `:805`, in `_values.py` after section 4.2;
`_auto_literal_conversion`; `decode_json_representation` in `provjson.py`;
`provrdf.py`'s `decode_rdf_representation` and `encode_rdf_representation`;
the PROV-N writer's literal output; `docs/reference/identifier.md:8`, which
describes `Identifier` as the class for `xsd:anyURI` values.

**Tests.** `attribute_values.py` is not appended: `generate.py` runs its
document under the 3.2.3 wheel, which cannot write a `Decimal`, a `date` or
`bytes`, so the converted types are tested in a module of their own that

### 5.8 Qualified names (#516, #551, #553, #599)

**3.3.0.** A qualified-name value that does not resolve is rejected: the reader
raises its error with the position, and the model raises
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName` for a value asserted from code or read from
a format whose reader has no position (RDF, PROV-JSONLD), as the how-to
already says of the PROV-JSONLD warning. Each caller of the section 4.3 checker
raises instead of warning, and `_reader_checking_names()` goes. A value that resolves
is a `QualifiedName` (5.7, D9).

**Resolution rule (D3).** Every reader resolves a qualified-name text by
splitting at the first `:` and looking the prefix up in the declarations in
scope, document or bundle, and never by matching a declared namespace IRI
against the text (3.2.3 D8, #599). This covers the positions #599 found and the
positions PR #581 listed as still silent in 3.2.3: in PROV-N a full IRI as an
identifier, a formal argument, an attribute name or a datatype; in PROV-JSON a
full IRI as an attribute name or a datatype, and a relation key with no prefix
and no default namespace. 3.2.3 did not warn about these, and `HISTORY.md`
says so. A plain string given as an identifier in code, such as
`bundle.entity("http://example.org/e1")`, still compacts, as the how-to
promises. The readers resolve through the model's resolver, so
`NamespaceManager._resolve_prefixed_string` compacts only when no scope
declares the prefix: when an enclosing scope declares it, the manager returns
`None` and `valid_qualified_name` delegates to that scope, instead of
compacting against its own namespaces (#599, amended 2026-10-22). The
PROV-JSON datatype position lands with #552 in 5.10, where
`_check_value_object` is rewritten (amended 2026-10-22).

The PROV-JSONLD context marks `type`, `role` and `location` as IRIs, so a bare
string there is an IRI, as a `{"@id": …}` node reference is: one a declared
namespace compacts is the `QualifiedName`, and one no namespace compacts is
stored as an `xsd:anyURI` `Literal` (5.7), where 3.2.3 kept an `xsd:QName`
`Literal` and warned under #516; `HISTORY.md` says so (amended 2026-10-22). A
value object typed `xsd:QName` or `prov:QUALIFIED_NAME` holding a full IRI, or
a name with an undeclared prefix, reaches the model and raises
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName` with the model's wording.

**Messages and machinery (amended 2026-10-22).** The section 4.3 checker
returns the sentence `the qualified name 'zz:x' of ex:k does not resolve,
because its prefix 'zz' is not declared`, without the outcome clause and the
issue number; the PROV-N reader raises it at the value token, the PROV-JSON
reader with the record and attribute, and the model as the `detail` of
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName(text, detail)`, so the model's message
repeats the name as #524's does. A PROV-N name that does not resolve keeps the
reader's `cannot resolve 'http://example.org/e': prefix 'http' is not declared`.
`ProvNParser.warnings`, `_future()` and `_reader_checking_names()` go;
`_rebuilding` and `_rebuild()` stay until 7.1, read by nothing but
`add_record()`'s own branch.

**Corpus.** `test_provn_corpus.py:99-100` reads Recommendation examples 34 and
35 under `pytest.warns` for an undeclared value prefix, and PR #581 counted
six such examples. W3C text cannot be fixed, so they are excluded with #516 as
the reason, in the corpus README's exclusion list, and the "Corpus evidence"
row of `docs/reference/conformance.md` ("127 files, 100 parse") changes.

**Site.** `records.py:831`; `provjson.py` lines 802 to 878 and the
`_check_name_value` call at 1115 with the `_check_record_key` callers;
`provn_parser.py` lines 862 to 899; the PROV-JSONLD reader's value-object path
(`provjsonld.py:536`); `Namespace._resolve_prefixed_string` and

**Tests.** The section 4.3 parity test, now asserting the error, in

## 9. Tests and verification

- Every 3.2.3 warning test has a successor that asserts the raise or the new
  behaviour, with the same input, and every "valid input does not warn" test
  stays.
- **Successor tests are table rows.** From PR 6 on, a reader's rejection
  tests are rows of one parametrised test per reader, `(input, error type,
  message, issue)`, in that reader's module, not one function per rule. A row
