# Release 3.3.0, spec 5: open defects, regressions and gaps

**Status:** Approved.

Written 2026-10-28 against `main` 4cbe06f (spec 4's PRs 24a and 24b merged; the freeze not
yet cut). Spec 1 is `2026-10-20-release-3.3.0-design.md`; its section 7.3 sets the release
A/B. Spec 3 is `2026-10-23-release-3.3.0-spec3-names-provn.md`; its sections 5.2, 6 and
13.5 are cited below. Spec 4 is `2026-10-27-release-3.3.0-spec4-performance.md`; its
section 6 and decisions S1, S6, S7, S11 and S12 are where most of the issues here came
from. Decisions in this spec are F1 to F23 (section 11).

Unless a sentence says otherwise, a figure was measured on `main` at 4cbe06f and on the
`prov==3.2.3` wheel under CPython 3.12.8 and rdflib 7.6.0 (Apple M2 Max, mains power), and
a benchmark figure is `benchmarks/ab.py <ref> --python 3.12`.

## 1. Purpose

3.3.0 has no deadline, so every open defect and every measured slowdown against 3.2.3
lands before the freeze. This spec settles what the issues leave open and names the pages
that change with them.

- **Readers and writers** (section 3). The PROV-JSONLD reader rejects a datatype no
  namespace resolves (#673); the PROV-O reader finds a blank-node subject's qualification
  (#674); the PROV-O writer builds a relation's qualification head once (#697); the PROV-N
  reader refuses a structural keyword where a name is expected (#683) and gains the
  corpus's 215 probes with two reader fixes (#685); the RDF property test excludes the one
  shape rdflib cannot write, and conformance caveat C11 says what that shape is (#701).
- **The model** (section 4). A read of a record no longer creates attribute entries
  (#696); the `hadMember` single-value rule follows the stored `prov:collection` (#700);
  the value key holds the datatype's URI in place of its name (#690); resolving an IRI
  against the registered namespaces is linear (#691); a namespace's name cache is bounded
  (#694).
- **Drawing and the bundle's document** (section 5). An unlabelled node drawn with
  `use_labels=True` shows its identifier once (#695); `prov_to_dot()` and `plot()` take a
  document, and a bundle argument warns (#652); `ProvBundle.document` and
  `ProvBundle(document=...)` are deprecated (#650).
- **Benchmarks** (section 6). The generator fills its five bundles and the GC setting is
  stated (#693), which reverses spec 4's S6.

#664 is closed without work; #677 (spec 3 PR 21c) fixed it (F1). #692, #667 and #684 are
out (section 10).

## 2. Evidence

### 2.1 Six issues read differently on `main`

- **#664 passes.** Its reproduction writes `prov:id="dn:a:b"` and
  `<prov:type xsi:type="xsd:QName">dn:a:c</prov:type>` and reads back as
  `http://d.example/a:b` and `['http://d.example/a:c']`. Commit 37ca0dd (#677) routes
  every PROV-XML name position through `_name_text()` (`provxml.py:559-576`), and a probe
  covering a literal datatype, a derivation's `prov:id` and `prov:ref`s, and a bundle
  `prov:id` with a record inside round-trips equal. `test_xml.py:1088` pins it. The issue
  was observed on afe126b, the day before 37ca0dd.
- **#701's failing shape is not a decimal `NaN`.** The example `.hypothesis/examples`
  replays is `entity(ex:e, [ex:k0="", ex:k1="NaN" %% xsd:double, ex:k1="0.000" %% xsd:decimal])`.
  The decimal `NaN` the issue describes is already excluded for `rdf` by the `assume` at
  `test_property_roundtrip.py:53` in `test_generated_record_roundtrips`, which is why 15,000 fresh examples passed. Probed on
  `main` and 3.2.3, identical:

  | `ex:a` holds | Turtle, TriG, N3 | N-Triples, N-Quads, RDF/XML, JSON-LD |
  |---|---|---|
  | `"NaN"^^xsd:double` beside `"0.000"^^xsd:decimal` | `decimal.InvalidOperation` | writes |
  | `float("nan")` beside `"0.000"^^xsd:decimal` | `decimal.InvalidOperation` | writes |
  | `"NaN"^^xsd:decimal` beside `0`, or beside `"1.5"^^xsd:double` | `decimal.InvalidOperation` | writes |
  | `"NaN"^^xsd:double` beside the plain `int` `1` | writes | writes |
  | `"NaN"^^xsd:double` beside `"1.5"^^xsd:double` | writes | writes |
  | any `NaN` alone | writes | writes |

  rdflib's Turtle family sorts the objects of one predicate by value
  (`turtle.py:155`), and Python raises when it orders a `Decimal` against a NaN of either kind (a `float` NaN
  against a `Decimal`, or a `Decimal` NaN against any number), while `NaN > float` and
  `NaN > int` return `False`. `test_value_forms.py:129-136` already pins the decimal NaN
  beside `0`.
- **#695 appears only with `use_labels=True`.** The comparison `record.label ==
  record.identifier` sits in that branch of `_add_node` (`dot.py:343-356`); the issue's
  call without the argument draws `ex:e` once. With `use_labels=True`, `ex:e` renders as
  `<ex:e<br /><font ...>ex:e</font>>`. The docstring of
  `test_use_labels_with_explicit_label_differing_from_identifier` (`test_dot.py:78-91`)
  records the unreachable branch as known, with stale line numbers.
- **#674 was a raise on 3.2.3.** The issue's Turtle raised
  `ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName` on rdflib's blank-node label in 3.2.3; the two
  records are `main`'s own unreleased reading (#639 made a blank-node subject resolve as
  an element's identifier does). The same miss sits twice: `_decode_relation_triple`
  stringifies the subject (`provrdf.py:1737`) and looks up `(URIRef(subj), prov:qualified*,
  None)` at 1760 and `(URIRef(subj), prov:asInBundle, None)` at 1746, where a `BNode` never
  matches. The only other `URIRef(subj)` (1812) builds a warning's text, and the `str(...)`
  keys at 1677 and 1778 index `state` dicts, not the graph.
- **#696's counts.** The reproduction gives 4 and 5 entries, not 2 and 4, because the peek
  also creates `prov:time` and `prov:label`; the order and the unequal PROV-JSON are as the
  issue says. The PROV-JSON and PROV-JSONLD writers iterate `record._attributes.items()`
  (`provjson.py:261-263`, `provjsonld.py:262`) and the PROV-N attribute block follows the
  same order, so a peek can change all three outputs; PROV-XML sorts its attributes and is
  unchanged. The write path has the same defect: `add_attributes` binds
  `self._attributes[attr]` (`records.py:1127`) before `_check_repeated_value` runs, so a
  rejected value leaves an empty entry. The comment that said so left in ba9208a; the
  behaviour stayed.
- **#697's reader side.** Reading either order back gives `prov:type [prov:Revision]`
  only, because a subject's `rdf:type` of its own class is the record type. An asserted
  `prov:type` equal to the record's own class never survives PROV-O, for any record type,
  with or without a subtype, on `main` and on 3.2.3: `entity(ex:e, [prov:type='prov:Entity'])`
  reads back as `entity(ex:e)`. The writer maps `prov:type` to `rdf:type`, so the asserted
  type and the record's type triple are one triple. No generated record in
  `strategies.py` carries a `prov:type`, so the property test never meets it.

### 2.2 Speed, re-measured after PR 24b

| Probe | `main` | 3.2.3 | Issue's figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| `flattened()`, 4,000 bundles each declaring a default (#692) | 1.65 to 1.67 s | 1.41 to 1.51 s (+12 to 17%) | +47% |
| the same at 1,000 | 0.10 s | 0.09 s | |
| PROV-O read, N-Triples, 2,000 one-record directories, no prefixes (#691) | 2.04 to 2.08 s | 1.96 s (+5%) | about +10% |
| the same at 500 | 0.21 s | 0.21 s | |
| GC-tracked objects per record, 2,000 entities of three attributes (#690) | 13.0 | 7.0 | 9.9 and 7.4 |

**#692.** PR 24b's constant-time `add_namespace` took the 4,000-bundle case from 2.09 s to
1.68 s, as the issue predicted. cProfile puts the rest in one chain on each side, about
8 million predicate calls (4,000 calls at an average depth of 2,000): on 3.2.3
`add_namespace` → `_get_unused_prefix` → `Identifier.__eq__`; on `main`
`_declare_prefixes_for_bundle_defaults` → `_unused_prefix_in_scope` → `_first_free` →
`_binding_in_scope` → `_binding` → `dict.get`, three Python frames deeper per probe. Both
are the same quadratic through `_first_free` (`namespaces.py:35-42`), which restarts at
`prefix` on every call. Nothing in `src/prov` ever unbinds a prefix (no `del`, `pop`,
`remove` or `clear` on a manager's bindings; only the resolve cache is cleared).

**#691.** cProfile at 2,000 directories (7.1 s profiled): `_resolve_prefixed_string`
scans every registered namespace for each new subject (`namespaces.py:355-358`, 2.0 s);
`_resolve_iri_via_graph` iterates rdflib's `namespaces()` for every IRI
(`provrdf.py:637`, 2 million iterations, 1.85 s); rdflib's own `compute_qname` and trie
insert for the minted prefix take 1.9 s; the resolve cache is cleared whole at four places, `_set_default` (`namespaces.py:136`),
`add_namespace` for a new namespace (201) and for a new prefix on a known URI (186), and
`_reconcile_qualified_name` on a rename (320), so every new namespace re-resolves even the
`prov:` and `rdf:` names. The first two are prov's and grow with the
namespace count; rdflib's share does not change with anything prov does. The resolve
cache holds hits only, keyed by the plain string (`namespaces.py:264-270`); `_uri_map` is
keyed by exact URI and written only in `add_namespace` (`namespaces.py:204`). The default
namespace sits in `self.values()` under the prefix `""` and matches only with a non-empty
local part (`namespaces.py:357`). The #599 rule runs before the scan: a prefix bound in a
parent scope returns `None` here (`namespaces.py:346-352`).

**#690.** The type histogram over the new GC-tracked objects puts the whole difference in
three `tuple`s and three `dict`s per record, one value key and one `TypedValueSet._index`
per attribute value. CPython untracks a tuple or a dict whose contents are all atomic
(`str`, `int`, `float`, `None`) at the first collection and keeps tracking one that holds
an instance; slot 0 of every key holds a `QualifiedName` today (`records.py:584-614`,
`Literal._key` at 530-541). The saving is for plain values (`str`, `bool`, `int`,
`float`, `datetime`), which the probe used; an `Identifier` value's key keeps its type and
the instance (602), and `_index` maps each key to the value itself (328), so a record whose
values are names or `Literal`s keeps its dict tracked whatever slot 0 holds. `QualifiedName` compares and hashes by URI
(`identifier.py:248-252`); no code reads a key's slots (keys are dict keys of `_index` and
members of the `_typed_attributes()` frozenset); `TypedValueSet.__setstate__` rebuilds
keys from values. `_value_key` restates the first five rows of `_PROV_FORMS`
(`_values.py:254-282`) as `isinstance` branches, and `_PLAIN_VALUE_BASES`
(`records.py:725-727`) and the `python_value_form` docstring list them again (#670).

### 2.3 The namespace cache (#694)

`Namespace.__getitem__` (`identifier.py:551-557`) memoises one `QualifiedName` per local
part, unbounded. `PROV._cache` holds 61 entries after `import prov.model` and `XSD._cache`
25; a PROV-O read adds 24 `prov:` names once per process. The readers reach the cache
through `NamespaceManager.lookup` (`namespaces.py:434`) on every name; the manager's own
`_resolve_cache` serves only plain-string `valid_qualified_name` calls. On the benchmark
document (8,000 records, 3,808 distinct names under `ex`), one cold process per phase:

| Phase | `ex` | `prov` | `xsd` |
|---|---|---|---|
| construction | 3,808 calls, 0% hits | 2 calls | none |
| PROV-JSON write | no lookups | | |
| PROV-JSON read | 18,805 calls, 80% hits | 1,250 calls, 100% | 2,500 calls, 100% |
| PROV-N read | 20,005 calls, 81% hits | 1,250, 100% | 1,250, 100% |
| PROV-O read, TriG | 8,369 calls, 55 to 68% hits | 1,302, 99.9% | 3 |
| PROV-O read, 2,000 minted namespaces | one key each, 0% | none | none |

The `prov` and `xsd` hits are vocabulary names written in the text (`xsd:int`,
`xsd:double`, `prov:SoftwareAgent`); the `ex` hits are relations naming entities created
nearby. A document's namespaces die with the document. The unbounded case is a
`Namespace` held for the life of a process, which `PROV` and `XSD` are and a user's
module-level namespace can be. Nothing relies on `ns["x"] is ns["x"]`.

### 2.4 The corpus probes (#685)

`expected.tsv`, a README; 33 KB). `expected.tsv` has the columns

closed issues). It does not distinguish warnings. 100 rows accept under both profiles,
100 reject under both, 15 reject under `strict` only. Sixteen rows carry `decision` in
their rule; every one agrees with `main` under both profiles. No probe holds a CR, a byte
order mark or a NUL. Read on `main`, 202 rows agree and 13 disagree, the same 13 under
both profiles:

The lexer unescapes a `NAME` before the parser checks `_PN_PREFIX_NAME` on the unescaped text

Recommendation says `\uXXXX` and `\UXXXXXXXX` "where X is a hexadecimal digit [0-9A-F]",
and production [57] `HEX`, which takes either case, belongs to `PERCENT`.

The existing corpus (`src/prov/tests/provn/`, 524 files) records no tag, keeps each
exclusion as `"file.provn": "reason"` in a module-level dict of `test_provn_corpus.py` with

pins every table with a guard test that each excluded file still fails; it uses no
`xfail`. `pyproject.toml`'s package-data lists each corpus subdirectory.

### 2.5 The PROV-N structural guard (#683)

`_reject_unclosed_structural_keyword(follow, expected)` (`provn_parser.py:278-298`)
refuses a bare `document`, `endDocument`, `bundle` or `endBundle` (`_STRUCTURAL`, line
168) unless the next token is in `follow`, raising without consuming it, so `_resync`
(492-516) stops at the keyword. #520 applied it to identifiers, arguments and extension
attribute names. Two reads lack it: the attribute name in `_attributes` (813-817), where
`_expect(TokenKind.NAME, ...)` consumes the keyword and `_resolve` then fails; and the
datatype after `%%` in `_literal_tokens` (837-841), which `_extension_pair` and
`_extension_argument` also reach. On `main`, the issue's document and its `[` variant give
`lenient` `expected 'endBundle', found end of input` at line 8 with no warning, and
`strict` and `default` `cannot resolve 'endBundle': no default namespace declared` at
line 5; the variant one token shorter already skips one statement under `lenient` and
raises `expected ']', found 'endBundle'` under the others. After an attribute name only
`=` may follow; after a datatype `,` or `]` in an attribute block and `,`, `)` or `}` in an
extension tuple or expression. `test_structural_keyword_shaped_data_still_parses` pins
`entity(bundle)` and `used(ex:a, endBundle, -)`.

### 2.6 The other readers' datatype rule (#673)

`_decode_value_object` (`provjsonld.py:552-566`) ends with
`Literal(text, bundle.valid_qualified_name(datatype_str))`; `valid_qualified_name` returns
`None` for an undeclared prefix, a full IRI no namespace covers, and `_:b1`, so all three
read as the plain text `'1'` with no warning. Six lines above, the #609 node-reference
path calls `resolve_name(bundle, text, attr=attr)` and raises `ProvJSONLDException` with
its sentence. The PROV-JSON reader rejects `{"$": "1", "type": "zz:t"}` with
`record 'ex:e', attribute 'ex:a': the qualified name 'zz:t' does not resolve, because its
prefix 'zz' is not declared` (#552), and the PROV-XML reader rejects `xsi:type="zz:t"` with
`line 3, <prov:entity> 'ex:e': Invalid Qualified Name: zz:t, the qualified name 'zz:t'
does not resolve, because its prefix 'zz' is not declared`, which ends in the same
sentence.

### 2.7 The `hadMember` rule and the readers (#700)

`add_attributes` scans the raw pairs for `name == PROV_ATTR_COLLECTION`
(`records.py:1130-1132`) before names are resolved and before `None` values are dropped.
No reader passes the string spelling. The PROV-O reader is the only one that passes
`(prov:collection, None)` to `new_record`: `_DecodeState.register` seeds every formal
attribute of a typed `prov:Membership` subject with `None` (`provrdf.py:386-390`), and
`_emit_decoded_records` walks a repeated `prov:entity` into one record per value (probed
on five shapes, including `ex:m a prov:Membership ; prov:entity ex:e1, ex:e2`, two records
with one entity each). The `hadMember` triple route goes through
`ProvBundle.membership(collection, entity)`, one pair per triple. No test passes
`(PROV_ATTR_COLLECTION, None)` or a multi-entity membership without a collection.

### 2.8 The bundle's document (#650, #652)

A bundle in a document holds `_document` and `_namespaces.parent`, set together
(`bundle.py:457-460`, 2043 and 2053). The back-reference is read at five places:
`add_bundle`'s ownership check (2030), `_unified_copy`'s standalone branch (859, marked to
go with `ProvBundle.unified()` in 4.0), the PROV-XML reader's `_adopt_own_default` (917)
and `_bare_name` (985), and `graph_to_prov` (`graph.py:194`). Four readers build
`ProvBundle(document=document)` before the identifier is known (`provjson.py:387`,
`provjsonld.py:809`, `provn_parser.py:443`, `provxml.py:454`). Four tests read
`.document` (`test_namespaces.py:194`, 243; `test_model.py:670`, 849); no docs page names it. `prov_to_dot(bundle)` and `bundle.plot()` emit the
`ProvBundle.unified()` deprecation on `main`, with a message about a method the caller did
not call (spec 3 section 13.5 left that deliberately). ProvToolbox's `Bundle` has
`getStatement()`, `getNamespace()` and an identifier and no document accessor; its
`Namespace` carries the `parent` link the readers set, and its writers thread the document
namespace down; `ProvToDot.convert(...)` and `ProvViz.build(...)` take a `Document` and
draw bundles through it, and validation runs on a `Document`.

A document that holds one bundle of another, for drawing it alone, is built from the
source's declarations: `d2 = ProvDocument(namespaces=d.get_registered_namespaces())`, the
default set from `d.get_default_namespace()`, then `d2.bundle(b.identifier).update(b)`.
Probed: the copy writes the bundle's own `prefix` and its default-namespace names
unchanged. Without the declarations, a default-namespace name raises
`ProvExceptionInvalidQualifiedName`, as spec 3 section 4.1 requires.

### 2.9 The benchmark generator (#693)

`build_document` (`benchmarks/conftest.py:33`) picks `bundles[i % 5] if i % 5 == 0 else
doc`, so every bundle record lands in `bundles[0]`: 8,000 top-level records and
`[2000, 0, 0, 0, 0]` at the default size. `ab.py` passes `--benchmark-disable-gc`
(`ab.py:48`) and the README does not say so; the issue measured GC on against off at
+10 to +43% on 3.11 and 3.12 across construction, the PROV-JSON read, `unified` and
`prov_to_graph`. `ab.py` runs both sides from the working tree's `benchmarks/`, so a
changed generator changes both sides' workload equally.

## 3. Readers and writers

### 3.1 PROV-JSONLD: a datatype no namespace resolves (#673, F7)

`_decode_value_object` resolves `@type` through the `resolve_name` call the node reference
uses, so an undeclared prefix, a full IRI and a blank node each raise
`ProvJSONLDException` with `the qualified name 'zz:t' of ex:a does not resolve, because its
prefix 'zz' is not declared` (for `_:b1`, the model's sentence for the reserved prefix
`_`). Before, the value read as plain text with the datatype dropped and no warning. A
blank node is not a datatype, so the #609 drop-with-warning does not apply. The rule is
the one the PROV-JSON and PROV-XML readers follow (2.6) and the settled one for every
reader (HISTORY, #675): a name resolves by its prefix in scope, never by matching a
namespace IRI against the text.

### 3.2 PROV-O reader: a blank-node subject's qualification (#674, F6)

Both `graph.triples` lookups in `_decode_relation_triple` take `subj_node`, the node
rdflib gave the reader, in place of `URIRef(str(subj_node))`: the `prov:qualified*` lookup
(1760) and the `prov:asInBundle` lookup for `mentionOf` (1746). A blank-node activity with
a binary `prov:wasAssociatedWith` and its `prov:qualifiedAssociation` reads as one
`wasAssociatedWith(<activity>, ex:ag, ex:plan)`, as the same activity under an IRI does;
a blank-node `mentionOf` subject finds its bundle. 3.2.3 raised on the input; `main`'s two
records never shipped. The change lands before the freeze.

### 3.3 PROV-O writer: the qualification head once (#697, F10)

`_encode_relation` builds the qualification head once per identified relation (the R3
form spec 4 measured and dropped under S12). `rdf:type prov:Derivation`, asserted as a
`prov:type` beside `prov:Revision`, `prov:Quotation` or `prov:PrimarySource`, survives in
every attribute order where `main` keeps it only when that value comes last (106 of 150
orders dropped it); `formal_objects` is filled once, so the predicate lookup is no longer
quadratic in one relation's attribute count. About three points off `serialize[rdf]`.

The reader side stays. An asserted `prov:type` equal to the record's own class is one
triple with the record's `rdf:type` in PROV-O, so no reader can tell the two apart (2.1),
and no fix exists short of adding `prov:type prov:Entity` to every entity read from PROV-O.
The conformance page records it as caveat C12 under "Edge cases": an asserted `prov:type`
equal to the record's own type is folded into its `rdf:type` and does not round-trip
through PROV-O; every other format keeps it. One test pins the loss, as
`test_value_forms.py` pins C11, so a change that makes it round-trip is noticed.

### 3.4 PROV-N: a structural keyword where a name is expected (#683, F11)

The guard of 2.5 applies at the attribute name in `_attributes`, with follow `(EQUALS,)`
and "an attribute name", and at the datatype in `_literal_tokens`, with follow `(COMMA,
RBRACKET, RPAREN, RBRACE)` and "a datatype", which covers the extension path through
`_extension_pair` and `_extension_argument`. For the issue's document and its `[`
variant, `lenient` skips the one statement with `PROV-N statement skipped: line 5, column
1: expected a datatype, found 'endBundle'` (or "an attribute name"), closes the bundle and
reads `ex:after`; `strict` and `default` raise that message at the same position where
they raised `cannot resolve 'endBundle': no default namespace declared`. `"x" %%
endBundle]` and `[endBundle=...` stay accepted under a `default` declaration, as
`used(ex:a, endBundle, -)` is (`test_provn_profiles.py:365` declares one; without one every
bare name fails to resolve).

### 3.5 PROV-N: the corpus probes and two reader fixes (#685, F12, F13)

**Vendoring.** `provn/probes/` from prov-corpus tag v2.2.0 (the `.provn` files and
`expected.tsv`) goes under `src/prov/tests/provn/probes/`, with the tag and source URL in
the corpus README and the glob in `pyproject.toml`'s package-data. `test_provn_corpus.py`
gains one test parametrised over the TSV rows times the two profiles that asserts accept
(records, or warnings) or reject (`ProvNSyntaxError`). The TSV is vendored unchanged; its
`provpy` column records where 3.2.2 differed and is not read by the test, since prov's own
view of each difference is the reasons dict below. The module gains a `KNOWN_PROBE_DIFFERENCES:
dict[str, str]` of the rows where prov differs by decision, each with its reason and the
issue or decision, excluded from the main test and pinned by a guard test that each still
disagrees, in the form the three existing tables take. The 16 `decision` rows need no
ruling; a later disagreement shows as a failing row. The conformance page's "Corpus
evidence" table gains a row for the probes.

**The ten documented differences** are the rows of 2.4 that cite #514, #512, N53 and #520.
The two unnumbered `xsd:dateTime` rows fall under #514's documented limit. The 64-deep

### 3.6 RDF: a NaN beside a decimal (#701, F2, F3)

## 4. The model

### 4.1 A read creates no attribute entry (#696, F9)

`get_asserted_types`, `get_attribute`, `args`, `formal_attributes`, `label`, `value`,
`get_startTime` and `get_endTime` look names up with `.get()` or iterate the existing
keys, and `add_attributes` creates an entry only when it stores a value (the existing set
fetched with `.get()` for the repeat check, the entry made after the check passes). A read
never changes a record, and PROV-JSON and PROV-JSONLD key order and the PROV-N attribute
block follow the order values were asserted.
3.2.3 behaves as `main` does.

### 4.2 The `hadMember` rule follows the stored collection (#700, F8)

The scan runs over the resolved, non-`None` pairs, so the rule that a membership holds
one `prov:entity` is lifted exactly when the record stores a `prov:collection`:
`"prov:collection"` as a string resolves and lifts it; `(PROV_ATTR_COLLECTION, None)` does
not, and two entities with it raise the existing message. The record's stored attributes
and the rule always agree. Only a direct call that asserts two entities and an explicit
`None` collection changes behaviour (2.7); no reader produces that shape.

### 4.3 The value key (#690, F14)

`_value_key` keeps its hand-written `isinstance` rows, and each plain row puts the
datatype's URI string in slot 0 where it put the `QualifiedName`. The four fixed URIs
(`xsd:string`, `xsd:boolean`, `xsd:double`, `xsd:dateTime`) are read once into module
constants; the `int` row returns `int_datatype(value).uri`, where `int_datatype` in
`_values.py` holds the size rule (`xsd:int`, `xsd:long` or `xsd:integer`) and
`canonical_xsd_datatype` calls it. `Literal._key` puts `self._datatype.uri` (or `None`) in
slot 0. An `Identifier` keeps `(type(value), value, None)` and the fallback row keeps
`type(value)`; slots 1 and 2 are unchanged everywhere. `QualifiedName` compares and hashes
by URI (2.2), so two names that were equal have equal URIs and two keys that were equal
stay equal. A plain value and its `Literal` twin share a key as before. Equality, hashing
and the per-attribute value set are unchanged, and a 3.2.x pickle rebuilds its keys as
before. For a plain value of an exact plain type the key tuple and the `_index` dict then
hold only atomic values and CPython untracks them, which takes the GC-tracked objects per
record from 13.0 to 3.2.3's 7.0. A subclass value (an `IntEnum` member, a `datetime`
subclass) sits in slot 1 and keeps them tracked, as does a name or `Literal` value. The
PR lands only if
`ab.py main` shows no slowdown on `construct`, `deserialize[json]` and `equality` (the
issue measured −0.7% to −2.8%); F14 records the run.

### 4.4 Resolving an IRI is linear (#691, F15)

Three parts, each exact by construction and stated as behaviour-free and minimal:

- An index of registered URIs by length beside `_uri_map`, kept at the two writes the scan
  reads through (`self[prefix] = namespace` at 203, which the rename path also reaches,
  and `self[""] = namespace` at 135), so `_resolve_prefixed_string` tests
  `iri[:n]` for each distinct length instead of every namespace. The scan's answer is the
  first match in registration order; when more than one length hits (nested namespaces
  such as `http://ex.org/` and `http://ex.org/a/`) the lookup falls back to the scan, so
  the two agree by definition. The default namespace keeps its non-empty-local-part rule,
  and the #599 parent-scope rule runs first, as it does today.
- `add_namespace`'s two clears (186, 201) drop from the resolve cache only the entries
  whose prefix part is the new prefix. The cache holds hits only; a hit came from a
  binding, which is never rebound (a clash renames the newcomer), or from the scan's first
  match, which a later registration cannot displace. `_set_default` (136) and
  `_reconcile_qualified_name` (320) keep clearing the whole cache; neither is on the
  read path the issue measures.
- `_resolve_iri_via_graph` builds the graph's bindings once per decode and scans that.

Prov's share becomes linear; rdflib's minting stays, so the 2,000-directory read goes from
about 2.05 s to roughly 0.7 to 0.9 s and the growth from 500 to 2,000 from 10x to about
5x. The PR ends with `ab.py main`, the 500 and 2,000-directory probe before and after,
and the complexity ledger, and merges only on the maintainer's weighing of the gain
against the maintainability cost (F15). The change lands before the freeze.

### 4.5 A bounded namespace cache (#694, F16)

`Namespace._cache` is bounded at 10,000 entries on every `Namespace`, with no special
case for `PROV` and `XSD`. The benchmark's largest namespace holds 3,808 names at 8,000
records, so an ordinary document never reaches the bound; a process-lived namespace does
and stays bounded at about 3 to 4 MB. Two policies are built and measured in the PR, and
the maintainer chooses on `ab.py main` (`deserialize[json]`, `deserialize[provn]`,
`construct`): an LRU on `collections.OrderedDict` (a `move_to_end` per hit, `popitem(last=False)`
past the limit), and a flush (clear the dict when it reaches the limit, then refill). Both
are behaviour-free: equality and hashing are by URI, and no code relies on
`ns["x"] is ns["x"]`. Pickling is unaffected, since `__getstate__` drops the cache and
`__setstate__` rebuilds it empty.

## 5. Drawing and the bundle's document

### 5.1 An unlabelled node under `use_labels` (#695, F4)

In `_add_node`'s `use_labels` branch, a record with no `prov:label` value takes the
one-line form (its identifier once) and a labelled record the two-line form (the label,
the identifier as subtitle). The branch today compares `record.label` (a `str`) with
`record.identifier` (a `QualifiedName`), which `Identifier.__eq__` never calls equal.
`docs/howto/graphics.md`'s sentence that an element without a label falls back to its
identifier then holds.

### 5.2 Drawing takes a document (#652, F20)

`prov_to_dot()` given a `ProvBundle` that is not a `ProvDocument` warns
`DeprecationWarning` (pass the document; 4.0 accepts documents only) and draws as it does
today, unified through the private `_unified_copy` so the `unified()` warning is not raised; `plot()` is documented on `ProvDocument`, and `ProvBundle.plot()` on a plain bundle
warns the same way. The `ProvBundle.unified()` warning that both emit today goes with it.
`docs/howto/graphics.md` and `docs/reference/dot.md` say "document", and the how-to shows
the one-bundle recipe of 2.8. With spec 3 section 13.5 this answers #652 with yes on all
three halves: `ProvDocument.unified()`, `prov_to_dot()` and `plot()` work on documents,
as ProvToolbox's drawing and validation do, and `_unified_copy`'s standalone branch keeps
its 4.0 note.

### 5.3 `ProvBundle.document` is deprecated (#650, F19)

`ProvBundle.document` and the `document=` argument of `ProvBundle()` warn
`DeprecationWarning` naming 4.0, with `deprecated:: 3.3.0` in their docstrings. The five
internal readers of 2.8 take the namespace parent: `add_bundle` compares
`bundle._namespaces.parent` with `self._namespaces`; the PROV-XML reader uses the document
it holds; `graph_to_prov` copies declarations from the parent manager; `_unified_copy`'s
use goes with its branch. The four readers, `ProvDocument.bundle()` (2077) and `_unified_copy` (856) set the
parent through a private path, a keyword-only `_parent` or `document.bundle()` once the
identifier is read, whichever the plan finds smaller, so prov raises none of the warning
itself. `add_bundle` keeps setting `_document` until 4.0, so the property still answers. A bundle then keeps one link to its document, the one that resolves
its names, and the two states #650 lists (a bundle that reports a document whose `bundles`
does not list it; a copy that reports a document that does not hold it) are gone with
the field in 4.0. ProvToolbox's model has the same shape (2.8). Nothing changes for a
caller who never touches the two names.
